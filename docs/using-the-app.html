Using the app

The app has five tabs: Live, Analyzer, OBS Reports, Settings and Help. Most of the time you only need Live and Analyzer.

Live tab

The banner at the top shows what's happening right now:

LightMeaning
GRAYNot watching. Click Start watching.
GREENWatching, but OBS isn't recording yet (or recording has stopped)
REDOBS is recording. The recording time is shown next to it.
AMBERThe recording is paused

Check OBS settings reads your current OBS setup and reports problems before you record. You can use it at any time, even when you're not watching.

Start watching / Stop watching turns live monitoring on or off. You can start it before opening OBS, and it connects when OBS appears.

The row of tiles shows live numbers: recording status, frames lost to rendering lag and encoding lag, OBS frame rate, GPU load, video-encoder load, CPU and the recording drive.

Advisor messages

Each message shows its type, the time, which phase it belongs to (before, during or after recording), and the position in the video when that applies.

TypeWhat it meansPop-up?
CRITICALYour recording is being damaged right now, or soon will be (for example, the drive stopped writing or space is running out)Always, unless pop-ups are off
WARNINGA real problem that affects quality, such as lost frames or a setting that will cause visible issuesWith the recommended setting
NEEDS YOUThe app needs something from you, such as a file it couldn't findWith the recommended setting
TIPWorth improving, but not harmful to this recordingNo
GOOD / STATUSEverything is fine, or a simple status updateNo

If the same problem keeps happening, the app updates one message and counts the repeats (for example “×12”) instead of flooding the feed. Use Problems and requests only to hide tips and status messages.

Message buttons

  • Show what to do / Hide steps shows or hides numbered steps that point to the exact OBS or Windows setting.
  • Pause recording / Resume recording pauses OBS so you can fix something without starting a new file. This only works while connected to OBS.
  • Open report opens the full report in your web browser. Copy link copies the report link so you can open it on another device or send it to someone.
  • Run deep scan scans the whole video frame by frame. See Deep scan.
  • Locate file / Browse for log appears when the app couldn't find a recording or OBS log, so you can point it to the right file.
  • Dismiss removes the message.

Analyzer tab

This tab is about your most recent recording. It shows the file name, when it started, its length and size, the OBS log it came from, and whether the app was watching at the time. If it wasn't, the analysis still checks the OBS log and the file. Turn on watching before your next recording to also see what the PC was doing at each problem.

  1. Click Analyze my last recording. Tick Deep scan first if you want a frame-by-frame check (see Deep scan). A progress bar shows each step, and Cancel stops it.
  2. The Last report box shows the verdict, the comparison with your previous recording, and the top findings.
  3. Open report shows the full report in your browser. Copy link copies its link. Open saved copy opens the copy saved on this PC, which works offline.

The Last report box stays, even after you close and reopen the app, until you analyze again. Nothing opens by itself.

OBS Reports tab

Saved reports lists every report you've made, newest first. Double-click one to open it, or use Open report, Copy link, Open saved copy, Reports folder or Delete.

Analyze an older recording from an OBS log is for recordings that aren't the latest one:

  1. Choose an OBS log. OBS writes a new one each time it opens; the newest are listed automatically. They are also in OBS under Help → Log Files → Show Log Files.
  2. Tick the recordings you want to check. If a file has been moved, double-click its row to point to it.
  3. Watching data is picked automatically when the app was watching during that recording.
  4. Click Analyze selected. The result appears on the Analyzer tab. To check a video without a log, use Check a video file only.

The report

Reports open in your web browser on this website’s report page. The whole report is packed into the link, after the “#”. Browsers never send that part to a server, so GitHub never receives your report; the page simply unpacks it in your browser. That means a report link works on any device, including your phone, and anyone you send it to can see it. Folder names are left out. Each report is also saved on your PC as a single HTML file that works offline. Reports contain:

  • Verdict and key findings, each with why it matters and how to fix it
  • Timeline of every incident, with clock time and video position, plus the likely cause based on what the PC was doing at that second
  • Chart of skipped frames against GPU, encoder, CPU and drive load
  • Settings check comparing OBS settings with what the file really contains
  • Windows events such as GPU driver resets, disk errors or USB disconnects
  • A short glossary of the terms used

Deep scan

A normal check reads the file's timestamps and takes seconds. A deep scan decodes every frame to find repeated or frozen frames. These are what lost frames often look like inside the file, along with the regular stutter caused by frame-rate mismatches.

It takes about 5–20 minutes per hour of 4K video and runs at low priority. Avoid starting one right before you play a demanding game.

Settings tab

  • OBS connection: a live status light (Connected, OBS not running, WebSocket off, or Password rejected) with a Check again button. The port and password are filled in from OBS automatically.
  • Alerts while you play: pop-ups for critical problems only, for warnings and critical (recommended), or off, plus an optional sound. Pop-ups are limited to one every 30 seconds, except critical ones.
  • Resource use: Normal checks once per second, and Light checks every 2 seconds and collects less detail.
  • Behavior: start watching when the app opens, keep running in the tray, and analyze automatically when a recording stops (turn this off to click Analyze yourself).
  • Guided setup: Run guided setup starts the walkthrough again. Ask about guided setup when the app opens brings back the question if you ticked Remember my choice.
  • Updates: shows your version, checks for updates when the app opens (you can turn this off), and has Check for updates now. Updates always ask first.

Where files are saved

Everything is in Documents\Live Recording Analyzer:

  • Sessions holds per-second PC data from each watching session. It's small: about 1–2 MB per hour.
  • Reports holds the saved reports (HTML) and a small file with each report's link.
  • settings.json holds your choices.
Live Recording Analyzer is free and runs entirely on your PC.
